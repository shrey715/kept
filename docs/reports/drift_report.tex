\takeaway{\textbf{The short version.} Ten features cross the drift line, but only one finding changes what we'd do.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{The big one:} the three payroll features are blank in every training row. That's deliberate: the payroll source is a
single snapshot, so filling past rows would leak the future. The model has never learned from Kept's core signal. The rules
and Kept sizing use it; the model doesn't. Fix: keep dated payroll snapshots and retrain.
\item \textbf{The loud but mostly mechanical ones:} cohort rates (PSI 5.5 and 4.0) and the delinquency features. Tonight is one
snapshot of the open book, while training is a year of decision points. Within each cohort the kept rate is only 1–3 points below
where training ended.
\item \textbf{A silent one:} \code{promise\_intent} is a model input that is blank in both training and live. Its PSI of 0
means ``nothing to compare'', not ``stable''.
\end{itemize}}

\section{How to read PSI}

PSI (population stability index) compares two distributions bin by bin:
$\sum (\text{live\%} - \text{train\%}) \times \ln(\text{live\%} / \text{train\%})$. Numbers are cut at the training deciles,
categories by value, and \textbf{missing values get their own bin}, so a feature that suddenly fills in or goes blank shows up
too. We use the usual bank bands: \textbf{below 0.10 stable}, \textbf{0.10–0.25 watch}, \textbf{0.25 or more drift}
(investigate, then decide on a retrain).

\section{Which features moved}

\fig[0.92\linewidth]{d_psi.png}{PSI of every registered feature, training vs tonight. Red is drift, orange is watch, blue is stable.}

\textbf{What the chart says.} The red bars fall into three groups, each with a different story.

\paragraph{Cohort rates: loud, mostly mechanical} \code{cohort\_kept\_rate} (5.46) and
\code{cohort\_recovery\_rate} (4.00) top the chart. Each is a cumulative rate per product × entry bucket, recomputed at every
point in time. In training that gives 3,229 distinct values drifting slowly through the year. Tonight there is one as-of date,
so there are only 6 values, one per cohort, and each lands in one or two training deciles. PSI punishes that heavily. The real
movement is small:

The direction is consistent, though. Across the training year the median rate slid from 0.64 (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) to 0.59 (Q3 2026),
and every cohort is a little lower again tonight. Promises are being kept slightly less often than a year ago. That's
worth watching, but it's not a reason to stop scoring.

\paragraph{Payroll features: the real finding} \code{payroll\_delay\_days}, \code{pay\_change\_vs\_3m\_avg} and
\code{estimated\_monthly\_spare\_cash} are blank in 100\% of training rows and filled for 20–25\% of tonight's cases. The
training build nulls them on purpose (\path{dbt/models/features/account_features.sql}): the deposit table is one snapshot
with no history, so using it for a decision made in March would leak September's data. The point-in-time rule held, at a cost:
\textbf{the model has never learned from payday or spare cash}, which is Kept's whole idea. The rules and Kept sizing use them
live; the uplift model can't.

\paragraph{Delinquency features: a different slice, as expected} \code{max\_dpd} (0.35), \code{balance\_at\_risk} (0.39) and
\code{days\_in\_bucket} (0.49) moved because tonight is the open book, not a stream of decisions. The median open case is 46 days
past due, against 29 at a training decision, and has \$437 at risk against \$135. \code{contacts\_last\_7d} (0.78) is the same
effect: a training row is a moment the bank acted, usually mid-cadence, while tonight includes every open case, most with no
contact this week (median 0, against 1).

payroll trio drops from 100\% blank to about 80\%. The text flags (\code{hardship\_signal}, \code{dispute\_flag},

decisions did. That fill-in is
why those three are in ``watch'' at 0.16–0.17, not because customers changed. The four dots stuck at 100\% on both sides
(\code{promise\_intent}, \code{promised\_date}, \code{promised\_amount}, \code{third\_party\_present\_flag}) are the silent
problem. Their PSI is 0 because there's nothing to compare. \code{promise\_intent} is a model input that has never had a value.

\section{What we do with this}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Retrain with payroll.} Keep a dated copy of the deposit snapshot each night. After about 90 days of snapshots,
the training build can fill payroll features point in time, and the model can finally learn the payday effect that the business
case is built on. Until then the effect lives in the rules and sizing, which is where it's measured anyway.
\item \textbf{Teach the check about cohorts.} Compare cohort rates to the last training value per cohort, with a tolerance
of a few points, instead of using decile PSI. Today's check cries wolf on them every night.
\item \textbf{Fill or drop the empty inputs.} The SetFit backfill only predicts hardship, so the promise fields can only come
from the LLM extraction, and 0 of the 67,039 rows in \code{features.text\_features} carry one. Either wire it through or take it out of the model's inputs. A feature
that's always blank is just noise in the registry.
\item \textbf{Keep the nightly gate.} \code{kept drift} runs after \code{kept features}. A drifted \emph{model input} that
none of the reasons above explains holds the new scores and pages the owner.
\end{enumerate}
