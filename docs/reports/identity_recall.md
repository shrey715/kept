# Identity: recall by injection

Generated by `scripts/identity_injection.py`. Precision is checked by hand: **99.3%** on 300 reviewed links (quality
report). Recall needs people we *know* should merge. The CRM's own duplicate pointer can't serve: the matcher uses it
as an exact rule. So we planted 200 new CRM records, each a copy of a real customer with **no national ID
and no duplicate pointer** (only the probabilistic matcher can find them), damaged one way, and ran the matcher on
the same 5% sample of people (by date of birth) with and without them.

| Planted duplicate | Merged with the original | Recall |
|---|---:|---:|
| exact copy | 39 of 40 | 98% |
| surname typo | 40 of 40 | 100% |
| no phone | 39 of 40 | 98% |
| moved (new postal code) | 40 of 40 | 100% |
| first initial only | 40 of 40 | 100% |
| **All** | **198 of 200** | **99%** |

**Missed:** 2, of which 0 joined somebody else; the rest stayed a person of their own, which a
steward can join (a missed link, not a wrong one).

**Nothing else moved, almost:** with the planted records taken back out, 2 of the clean run's
50,036 clusters aren't exactly as they were. The matcher is retrained on each run's records (its
weights come from the data), so a borderline pair can land the other side of the threshold.
