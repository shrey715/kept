\section{TL;DR}\label{tldr}

\textbf{Base case: \$2.2M a year} (low \$1.0M, high \$4.2M) against
\textbf{\$150K a year to run} and \textbf{\$300K to productionise}.
First-year ROI \textbf{3.8x} in the base case (1.3x low), payback in
about \textbf{2 months} (4 months low).

Where it comes from, biggest first:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Promises dated to payday.} Promises due 0--2 days after payday
  get \textbf{62.5\%} of the promised money paid; 8+ days after,
  \textbf{51.5\%}. Kept sizing puts the due date right after payday.
\item
  \textbf{A cheaper, kinder contact mix.} Same number of calls, but most
  become hardship reviews instead of collection calls, and \$1.10
  letters become near-free texts, emails and app pushes.
\item
  \textbf{Agent time.} Auto call summaries cut after-call work on
  629,435 agent calls a year.
\item
  \textbf{QA time.} Every call gets pre-scored, so reviewers listen to
  the ones that need it.
\end{enumerate}

Plus an upside we don't count in the base case: \textbf{every point of
roll rate is worth \textasciitilde\$603K a year} in charge-offs avoided.
The 5\% hold-out measures it after launch.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{The gap:} weighting by where the money sits today, moving
  promises that fall 3+ days after payday to 0--2 days lifts the paid
  share by \textbf{5.9 points}.
\item
  \textbf{The base:} \textbf{\$41.7M} promised in the 12 months to 28
  Sep 2026.
\item
  \textbf{We only take half of it} in the base case
  (\texttt{PAYDAY\_CAPTURE} = 50\%): the gap is descriptive, and some of
  it is who gets which due date, not the date itself. That gives
  \textbf{\$1.23M a year}; the full gap would be \$2.46M.
\end{itemize}

\section{2. Contact mix and cost}\label{contact-mix-and-cost}

\includegraphics[width=1\linewidth,height=\textheight,keepaspectratio]{docs/reports/figures/bc_mix.png}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Calls:} 40,601 call-type actions for Kept vs 41,574 for the
  current strategy. Basically the same number, but \textbf{37,409 of
  ours are hardship reviews} (a specialist conversation) where the old
  strategy has 34,326 collection calls.
\item
  \textbf{Letters → digital:} the old strategy sends 7,180 letters at
  \$1.10; Kept uses SMS links (\$0.01), email and app pushes (\$0.001).
\item
  \textbf{Cost per pass over the book} (channel costs from
  \texttt{contact\_history}, Sep 2026): \$80,927 old vs \$71,187 Kept,
  \textbf{\$9,740 saved each pass}. At 52 passes a year (base) that's
  \textbf{\$506K}.
\item
  \textbf{Model's view:} tonight's 55,674 positive-uplift proposals are
  expected to bring in \textbf{\$2.06M more within 14 days than waiting}
  (uplift × balance at risk). It's a model estimate, so it's not in the
  totals: the hold-out proves or disproves it.
\end{itemize}

\section{3. Roll rate → charge-offs (the
upside)}\label{roll-rate-charge-offs-the-upside}

\includegraphics[width=1\linewidth,height=\textheight,keepaspectratio]{docs/reports/figures/bc_funnel.png}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{48.8\%} of cases that enter at 1--30 days past due have rolled
  by day 30 (330,779 cases since 2016).
\item
  Of rolled cases, \textbf{2.48\%} end up charged off, averaging
  \textbf{\$12,523}. Of cases that don't roll: \textbf{0.00\%}. Rolling
  is the only road to a charge-off.
\item
  \textbf{194,515 cases (\$63.9M overdue)} entered at 1--30 DPD in the
  last 12 months, so \textbf{1 point of roll rate ≈ \$603K a year}
  avoided.
\item
  The design's hypothesis is 1--2 points. We keep it \textbf{out of the
  base case} because it overlaps the payday gain (kept promises are what
  stop rolls), and count 1 point in the high case only.
\end{itemize}

\section{4. Time saved}\label{time-saved}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Call summaries:} 629,435 agent calls in 12 months, average
  handle time 6.8 min. Saving 60 s of after-call work each at \$36/h is
  \textbf{\$378K a year} (about 5.8 agents' worth of hours back on the
  phones).
\item
  \textbf{QA:} a 2\% manual sample at 12 min a call is 2,518 reviewer
  hours a year. Pre-scoring every call (88--100\% agreement with human
  QA per item) lets reviewers skip the clean ones: \textbf{\$45K} at
  50\% time saved, and coverage goes from 2\% of calls to all of them.
\end{itemize}

\section{6. Things that don't fit in a
spreadsheet}\label{things-that-dont-fit-in-a-spreadsheet}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Fair treatment and regulator risk.} Hardship shows up when the
  customer \emph{says} it (text) or the money \emph{shows} it (late
  payroll, a pay cut, new EI), and it immediately limits contact to
  protective actions, with two people releasing anything sensitive.
  Every decision has a plain-English reason and an audit trail.
\item
  \textbf{The cost of the protection, honestly.} 38,115 of tonight's
  cards are Red (a specialist reviews first). That's real specialist
  time. A rule for customers already on an active hardship plan would
  cut it; it's the next thing we'd ship.
\item
  \textbf{Measured, not promised.} A permanent 5\% hold-out stays on the
  old strategy, so every number above gets checked against reality, and
  a kill switch reverts everyone if it isn't working.
\end{itemize}
